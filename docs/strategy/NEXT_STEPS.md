# Moko: Next Steps to Refine the Business

> Draft for team review, 2026-09-27. Companion to [STRATEGY.md](STRATEGY.md), [MARKET_AND_REVENUE.md](MARKET_AND_REVENUE.md) and [QA_PREP.md](QA_PREP.md).

The revenue model shows the business depends on three things we haven't tested: that makers will pay, that they won't cancel between products, and that we can collect commissions. Everything below is aimed at those three.

## 1. Validation steps, in order

1. **Talk to 15 hobby makers who already have a prototype.** Find them on r/3Dprinting, MakerWorld and maker Discords, and ask about the last product they tried to get made and where it stalled. If fewer than about half have a real stuck product, the beachhead is wrong.
2. **Check our cost estimates against real quotes.** Run 10 real parts through Moko, then get actual quotes from Xometry and a few shops. If most aren't within about 25%, fix that before launch, because wrong numbers cost creators money.
3. **Sign 3–5 real manufacturers who'll take 100–500 unit runs.** The production commission is the revenue stream that makes the model work, and today every shop in the app is fictional.
4. **Test the price before building billing.** Offer a founding Pro plan at $19–29 a month with a real payment link, and see if at least 10 people pay. Also test a one-time "production pack" per product, since makers may only launch once or twice a year.
5. **Each of us takes our own hobby project to production with Moko, and posts every step.** It proves the product works, gives us our first case studies, and is the distribution plan.
6. **Hand-broker the first 3 production runs**, charging the planned commission, before building order routing into the app.
7. **Decide the open questions:** the beachhead, monthly vs. per-product pricing, which commission to start with, and whether we allow sponsored manufacturer spots.

Hold off on new features until steps 1–4 come back. Their results go straight into the spreadsheet's yellow cells and tell us whether we're in the low, base or high case.

## 2. Business refinements, most important first

1. **Positioning in one sentence.** Pick it and use it everywhere. For example: "Moko turns your hobby prototype into a product you can sell, with real costs, real manufacturers and a launch plan." The docs describe eight features; customers buy one outcome.
2. **Pricing structure.** Makers may launch only one or two products a year, so a pure monthly plan invites cancelling between launches. Offer three options: free design, a per-product "Launch pack" (e.g. $99 once), and Pro at $29 a month for people with several products. Test which one sells.
3. **Make the commission simple.** One headline number creators understand, such as "5% on production runs you order through Moko, nothing on your sales." Stacking a sales commission on top of Etsy's ~10% feels like double charging, so sales commissions apply only on Moko's own pre-order pages.
4. **Own the money flow on production orders.** To collect a commission, creators pay through Moko: we hold the payment, pay the manufacturer on delivery (Stripe Connect handles this), and keep our cut. That's also a selling point, since it protects first-timers from a bad factory.
5. **Build the manufacturer network as a business, not a feature.** A simple partner agreement (commission or referral fee, response time, quality standard) and a vetting checklist. Start with 5 shops that take small runs. Sponsored placements come later, once we send them real orders.
6. **Liability and trust.** Our cost figures are estimates and we don't make the product, so the terms of service must say that clearly. Add a basic dispute process for production orders, and offer NDAs before a creator shares full files with a manufacturer.
7. **Set up the company properly.** Form an LLC or C-corp (C-corp if we plan to raise), with a founders' agreement covering equity split, vesting and who owns the code and brand. Check the Moko name for trademarks and domains, which hasn't been done.
8. **Track 3 numbers weekly:** free-to-paid conversion, monthly cancellations, and production runs per paying creator. They decide which case of the model we're in.
9. **Decide how to fund it.** In the base case we need to cover about $80–90K of losses before monthly profit around month 23. Options: bootstrap with unpaid founders longer, pitch competitions and grants for student founders, or a small pre-seed round once we have 50 paying creators and a few real runs.
10. **Name the moat.** Every real quote and completed run makes our cost estimates better, which competitors can't copy without the same order history. Lead with that when pitching.
